Privacy Policy

Last updated October 15, 2026

Haystack is played outside, so it needs your location while you play. We collect as little as we can, keep it for as short a time as we can, and only show it to the people you are playing with. Here is exactly what happens.

The short version

  1. Your location is only tracked while a game you are in is running. Nothing is tracked in the lobby, between games, or when you are not in a game.

  2. Only the players in your game can see anything about it, and what they see depends on the game (see Who sees what).

  3. Location data is deleted 30 days after the game. Game photos are deleted once all players have left the recap, or 24 hours after the game ends, whichever comes first.

  4. We do not sell your data, we do not use it for advertising, and there are no ad or usage-analytics SDKs in the app. The only reports the app sends are crash reports, described below.

What we collect

Account. A random user ID, your display name and color. If you save your account with Sign in with Apple: the identifier Apple gives us for you, the email address Apple shares (a private relay address if you choose Hide My Email), and, the first time only, your name if you share it. Guests share none of these.

Location during games. Each position your phone reports, with its accuracy, speed and time, and whether the phone says the position was simulated. The app keeps sending positions while it is in the background or your phone is in your pocket, because a game does not stop when the screen turns off. iOS shows the location indicator while it does. Positions are used to run the game: checking challenges that depend on where you are, showing teams on the map where the game does that, keeping play inside the game area, preventing cheating and drawing the replay.

Positions outside a game. When you host a game, your current position is sent once to set where the game is played. Uptown also checks your position once when you start or join, to confirm you are in one of its cities. Your position if you press panic.

Game data. The games you joined, your team or role, challenges and their results, cards, votes, scores, and the photos you take for challenges. Photos are taken with the in-app camera. The recap can save the game's photos to your phone. The app only adds photos to Photos and never reads your photo library.

Purchases. Purchases are switched off for now, so we collect nothing about them. If paid passes come back, we will record which pass you bought, whether it is active and when it expires, as reported by RevenueCat. We never see your payment details.

Push token. If you allow notifications, your phone gets a token (an address for sending to it) from Apple and Expo. We save it to your profile so we can send game notifications, such as a challenge starting or a vote opening. We use it for nothing else. If you do not allow notifications, there is no token.

Crash reports. If the app crashes or hits an error, it sends a report to Sentry: what went wrong, the app and iOS versions, the phone model, the screen you were on and the last few steps before the error. About 1 in 10 sessions also sends screen-load timings. Reports carry your random user ID, so we can tell whether an error is one phone or many. They do not carry your name or email. Locations and request contents are stripped out before anything leaves your phone, and the app sends no screenshots or screen recordings.

Safety. That you accepted a game's safety briefing, and when. Which players a host removed from a lobby.

We do not collect your contacts, your photo library, advertising identifiers, or usage analytics.

Who sees what

Uptown. Everyone in the game, on both teams, sees each player's latest position on the map during the race. After the game, its players can see a replay of everyone's route.

Inside Job. Nobody sees anyone's position, during the game or after it. Your location is only used to check challenges, for example that the group walked far enough. Your secret role is shown only to you (and a Traitor sees the other Traitors) until the game ends, when everyone's role is revealed.

Hide and Seek and Courier (in testing, not yet open to everyone). The hunting side sees each other's positions. The hiding side's exact position is never shown to the hunters during the game: the server works out each answer and sends only the result, such as "within 1 km: yes". Some rules show a rough area instead, and a hider can send a photo when asked. After the game, its players can see a replay.

Photos. A photo you take for a challenge is shown to the players in that game.

Panic. If anyone presses panic, the game ends for everyone. That player's location is shown to every player in the game, and their app can update it for up to 30 minutes afterwards. It stays visible to those players in the recap until it is deleted with the rest of the game's location data.

Other players see your display name and color in the games you share. Nobody outside a game can see anything about it.

How it is used

Only to run the game. That covers checking challenges and answers on our servers, keeping time, sending game notifications, showing replays to the players of that game, preventing cheating and abuse, and providing support. Where the GDPR applies, we process this data because it is necessary to provide the game you asked for (contract), and to keep players safe and the game fair (legitimate interests).

Who we share it with

These service providers run Haystack on our behalf and may only use the data to provide their service to us:

  • Supabase: our database, sign-in, file storage and servers. Everything above is stored there.
  • Apple: Sign in with Apple, if you use it. Apple confirms who you are to us; we never see your Apple ID password.
  • Expo and Apple Push Notification service: deliver game notifications. They receive your push token and the notification text, which can include a player's display name (for example "Sam pressed panic").
  • Sentry: receives crash reports (see What we collect). It gets your random user ID, but no locations, name or email.
  • RevenueCat: only if paid passes come back. It would process purchases with Apple and receive your random user ID and purchase details, but not your location or game data.
  • Our basemap provider, currently OpenFreeMap: serves the map tiles and labels your phone displays. Like any website, it sees your IP address and the map area you are looking at, but not who you are or anything about your game.

OpenStreetMap. Some games need nearby places, such as stations, parks or landmarks. Our server asks the public OpenStreetMap Overpass service for them in a box around the game area. The request carries only that box. It never includes who is playing, any player's position, or anything from your phone. Your phone does not contact Overpass. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License.

We disclose data if the law requires it. We do not sell or rent personal data, and we do not share it for advertising.

How long we keep it

Data How long
Location points from a game An hour after the game ends we thin them to about one every 30 seconds for the replay. Deleted 30 days after the game ends, or 30 days after they were recorded if that is sooner
Other positions in a game (where it was played, off-limits circles, panic location) Deleted 30 days after the game ends
Game photos Deleted once all players have left the recap, or 24 hours after the game ends
Map data loaded for a game (OpenStreetMap places) Deleted 2 days after the game ends
Game results (who played, teams and roles, challenge results, votes, scores, cards) Kept so players' history and recaps work. A deleted account is removed from them
Profile, push token, purchase status Until you delete your account
Crash reports (held by Sentry) Up to 90 days, then Sentry deletes them. Deleting your account does not remove reports already sent, which carry only your random ID

Every game ends on its own after 8 hours at most. Once the 30 days are up, a game's recap still shows who played and the results, but no routes or positions.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time in Settings → Delete account. This takes effect immediately.

  • Deleted: your profile, your email address, our access to your Apple ID, your push token, your location history, your photos, your place in every game.
  • Games: lobbies you host end. Other live games carry on without you, and if you were the host, the next player to have joined becomes the host. Finished games stay in other players' history without you.

Deleted data is removed from our live systems straight away and from backups within 30 days.

Your choices and rights

You can:

  • play as a guest, without signing in
  • decline notifications
  • refuse or turn off location (games ask again when they need it)
  • edit your name and color
  • delete your account at any time

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR, UK GDPR or CCPA) you may have rights to access, correct, export or delete your data, and to object to or restrict processing. Email haystackapp.help@gmail.com and we will respond within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Haystack is for people aged 18 and over, and asks you to confirm your age before you play. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone younger. If you think a child is using Haystack, email us and we will delete the account.
